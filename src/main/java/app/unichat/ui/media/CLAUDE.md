# ui.media — notes for Claude

Photos, video, audio and animations on screen: `AnimatedImage` + `AnimationBudget`, `ImageCache` and `Stills`, `MessageMedia` (a message's media in the timeline), `MediaViewer` (+ `PhotoPane`, `PhotoZoom`, `Gallery`), `VideoViewer` (+ `VideoControls`), `InlineVideo`, `MediaPlayers`, `AudioPlayback` (+ `AudioControls`, `AudioBar`), `MediaOpening`, `MediaSaver`, `MediaTile`, `MediaSizes`, `FilePreview`, `ProfileMedia` (the info panel's Multimedia: `ui/chats/CLAUDE.md`). Conversions are `video/` (`video/CLAUDE.md`, which points here); the sticker and GIF grids with their `TileCache` are `ui.emoji`.

- Video: `Message.playsInline` decides by what the bridge reports (GIF flag, video stickers, or up to 2 MB and 15 s); `InlineVideo` (muted loop in the timeline, a native player only while on screen, none with reduced motion), `VideoViewer` (window; downloads on opening) with `VideoControls` (±10 s, volume, speed, repeat, watch again, full screen with controls that hide while playing, always on top, save the video or the frame; YouTube's keys: K J L, ←→ ↑↓, M, F, < >, , ., Home/End, digits). javafx-media comes with javafx-web; WebM it can't play goes through `video/WebmStickers` (ffmpeg to animated WebP or, if that ffmpeg lacks the encoder like Homebrew's, GIF; cached in `cache/media`; `video/Ffmpeg` uses the person's own or downloads a pinned build on first use, `video.ffmpegAuto`, Ajustes → Redes), `VideoViewer` converts other unreadable videos to H.264 (`video/VideoTranscoder`), and what still can't play falls back to a file chip and the system player. Trust the file, not its label: a sticker labelled `video/webm` may be delivered as a GIF; `.mov` is cached as `.mp4` because JavaFX refuses that name. Reels that arrive as a picture + `external_url` open their page (`Message.isReelLink`).
- Photos: `MediaViewer` (window and controls; steps through the chat's pictures with ← → / Page Up/Down and the side buttons, `Gallery`: photos and pictures sent as documents, which open there too; the arrows pan while zoomed in; F full screen, Esc leaves it then closes) over `PhotoPane` (mouse) and `PhotoZoom` (pure: scale, turn, position). Animated GIF/WebP stay decoded at up to 1600 px, so real size there is the decoded one.
- A still WebP over 3 MP (a phone's photo) is never decoded in Java: TwelveMonkeys takes ~25 bytes/pixel, 600 MB+ for 24 MP; `video/BigPictures` makes a 1600 px PNG with `sips` (Mac) or ffmpeg, cached (`AnimatedImage.isBigWebp`, read from the header).
- Audio: `AudioPlayback` (one per window: starting another stops it; survives changing chats), shown by `AudioControls` in the message and in `AudioBar` on top. JavaFX refuses Ogg/Opus: `stt/PlayableAudio` makes a 48 kHz WAV once (`OggOpus.toPlayableWav`; 16 kHz is only for Whisper).
- Animations: one over `AnimatedImage.MAX_ANIMATION_BYTES` first loses resolution, down to its size on screen (`shrinkToKeep`), and only then frames: dropping them halved a sticker's frame rate. Media grids keep their tiles (`emoji/TileCache`) and `MessageMedia.loadMedia` shows what `AnimatedImage` already decoded at once.

- A picture loading (a chat's sticker or photo, a panel's sticker or GIF) shows it as it really goes: `MediaLoading.track` puts a `ProgressRing` over its box with the share that arrived (`MediaSource.media(…, progress)`; bridges send the size), going round only while that's unknown, and nothing for what was already here; when it couldn't be downloaded or opened (`MessageMedia.loadMedia`'s future fails on either), a button tries again (a click on it never reaches the box, which would send or open it). `MediaLoadingUiTest`.

## Lessons
- Never hand a GIF to `new Image(...)`: JavaFX animates it with a Timeline that never stops and holds every decoded frame, so each GIF ever shown stayed in the heap (OutOfMemoryError in a day with Discord). Animations go through `AnimatedImage.decodeGif/decodeWebp` + `play`; thumbnails/avatars through `AnimatedImage.still` (with direct media a "thumbnail" can be the original animated GIF).
- Stickers never animated: images loaded the server *thumbnail* (a static PNG of frame 1), and JavaFX can't read WebP at all. Stickers/GIF/WebP load the original; animated WebP is composited from its ANMF chunks (TwelveMonkeys only returns raw patches without durations; `AnimatedImageTest`, synthetic fixture). Decoding long stickers takes seconds: show the thumbnail first. Reduce-motion shows frame 1 only.
- An OutOfMemoryError in the render thread freezes the window for good (the FX thread waits for a frame that never finishes, `PaintCollector.waitForRenderingToComplete`) while the sync and server keep going. The cause was iPhone photos as WebP (24 MP). Measure peak heap with real files (`-Xmx256m` and a folder of them) before trusting a decoder with someone else's pictures.
- TwelveMonkeys gets some lossy animated WebP frames' colours wrong (black blotches): `video/AnimDump` (libwebp's `anim_dump`) decodes them, TwelveMonkeys is the fallback.
- What a picture declares is a stranger's choice: a few hundred bytes of animated WebP can declare 300 frames of 8000x8000, and anim_dump wrote every frame to disk before any cap applied. `AnimatedImage.isBigWebp` refuses an animation past `MAX_ANIMATION_PIXELS` (canvas × frames, from the headers) like a phone's photo: it goes to `BigPictures`, in another process (`BigWebpTest`).
- Animations playing at once share `AnimationBudget` (192 MB of frames): past it a sticker or GIF shows its first frame until another leaves the scene. Without it a sticker-heavy chat ran a 1 GB heap out and froze the app (`src/main/java/app/unichat/CLAUDE.md`, Recovery). A new place that animates goes through `AnimatedImage.play`, never its own `Timeline` over decoded frames.
- Avatars flashed on every inbox refresh: recycled cells hid the photo and reloaded it (a background-loaded `Image` is blank until decoded). Then, cached per size, one contact at 44/34/28/84 px still missed the cache on every click. Now `ImageCache` keeps one decoded 168 px image per URL for every size, loads are de-duplicated, failed URLs aren't retried per click, and the 150 most recent chats' photos are preloaded (`AvatarFlashUiTest`).
- "Open the video" was written three times and only the timeline's paused the window's audio: a video opened from the info panel played over the voice note. One place opens media outside the chat (`MediaOpening`, `ProfileMediaUiTest`).
- A reel the Instagram bridge gives only as its picture and link (`Message.isReelLink`: `m.image` + an `instagram.com/reel/` `external_url`; many arrive so, others as `m.video` and play as any video) opened Instagram. A click plays it in `VideoViewer` now (`MediaOpening.reel`): `autosave/LinkVideo` fetches it with the saving engine (yt-dlp, the person's cookies, H.264) once into `cache/media` (a real reel: 2 MB, ~14 s the first time); only when that fails does the player close and the page open.
- A picture in a message's HTML (an embed, a link preview) started as a 16:9 box and grew to its shape once loaded, moving the chat, again after every restart. Its box now starts at its real shape when known: the `<img>`'s `width`/`height` (`matrix/MessageHtml` writes a preview's `og:image:width/height`), else the shape seen last time (`PictureShapes`, `cache/picture-shapes.tsv`, the newest 4000), else 16:9 (chat apps' rule: reserve the space before it loads). JavaFX's `VirtualFlow` already keeps the bottom in view while following and the message being read in place when something above grows (`AutoScrollUiTest`).
- A custom reaction showed ✨ for ever (CI's Linux, `ReactionEmojiUiTest`): `ImageCache.load` chained "remove me from `LOADING`" inside `LOADING.computeIfAbsent`, and a small picture decoded before the chain was built ran that remove inside the map's own compute, which `ConcurrentHashMap` refuses ("Recursive update"): the load failed and stayed in `LOADING`, failed, for good. Never touch a map from inside its own `compute*`; register the future first (`putIfAbsent`), then start the work (`ImageCacheUiTest` decodes on the calling thread to make it certain).
- A favourite that is a WebM (Telegram's video stickers) never loaded in the side panel: the chat converted WebM (`showConverted`), but `loadMedia`, which the panel's tiles use, handed it to the picture decoder ("Invalid image") and offered to try again for ever. `loadMedia` converts a WebM first too (`WebmStickerTileUiTest`). A new place that shows a sticker goes through `loadMedia`, never its own decode.
- The loading ring's arc sat as a ")" in the middle of its circle and spun around itself: a StackPane centres each child's bounds, and a quarter arc's bounds aren't the circle's. Shapes that must share a centre go in one `Group` (positioned by their own coordinates), turned with a `Rotate` about that centre, never `RotateTransition` (which turns about the node's bounds) (`ProgressRingUiTest`).
- Unichat restarted itself (memory full) with a big Telegram sticker set open: `AnimationBudget` only counted animations moving, while every `play` kept all its frames in its Timeline, moving or not (scrolled away, past the budget): 66 real stickers held 410 MB. Now only what's on screen (a clip, a ScrollPane's viewport, counts) moves and holds frames; the rest keeps the picture it shows and reads its frames again from its file (`Frames.source`) when it comes back; the decoded cache is 64 MB and the budget 128 MB. The same 66: 208 MB, flat whatever the set's size (`OffScreenAnimationUiTest`). Measure with real files and a real-size viewport: a tall test window showed everything and hid the difference.
- Stickers and GIFs behind a sheet (forwarding, any overlay card) flashed black and drew wrong on the Mac: every frame under the card's 40 px `dropshadow` made JavaFX render the effect again over changing content. Behind an open overlay animations stand still (`AnimatedImage.cover`, called by `MainView.showOverlay`/`hideOverlay`; what's inside the overlay still moves), the inline videos too, told at once (`AnimatedImage.seen`, `watchCover`: the first fix forgot `InlineVideo`, a native player, and Telegram's GIFs, which are MP4s, kept flashing): they're dimmed anyway, and it saves the work (`CoveredAnimationUiTest`). Headless snapshots can't show the flicker (they render whole frames); only the real window does.
- Video stickers (WebM) were made a GIF to be shown (`WebmStickers.animated`): from a 100 KB WebM, GIFs of 8 to 21 MB, 2-5 s each, decoded again; the panel full of them restarted Unichat for memory. They're decoded straight into frames now (`AnimatedImage.decodeVideo` → `video/VideoFrames`: ffmpeg writes raw RGBA at the size shown to a pipe), with a GIF's caps; no file in between, and no WebM is ever made a GIF (`VideoFramesTest`).
- A WebP showed only a retry button: TwelveMonkeys' VP8 decoder ran out of data on a still one libwebp reads fine (Discord's preview of a link). `composeWebp` falls back to libwebp's anim_dump when the Java decoder fails, still pictures too (`AnimatedImageTest.aWebpTheJavaDecoderCantReadIsReadByLibwebp`).
- Opening the sticker panel, and sending a Telegram sticker to Discord, restarted Unichat for memory though what stayed was ~200 MB: the peak was each decode holding a sticker's frames whole before making them small (anim_dump's 512 px PAMs, all at once; a video's raw output in one growing buffer, then a copy). Frames are made small as they're read (`AnimDump.frames(file, each)`), a video's come one at a time through a reused buffer (`VideoFrames.read(…, Frame)`, `support/Processes.read`), and animations being decoded share `DecodeBudget` (96 MB; past it they wait; one bigger goes alone) (`DecodeBudgetTest`, `AnimDumpTest`, `VideoFramesTest`). Measure with the app's own tools: a probe without anim_dump measured TwelveMonkeys instead. Measured: 148 real stickers at 512 px needed a 768 MB heap before, 512 MB after; a JavaFX image takes ~1.4 times the 4 bytes a pixel the caps count.
- An animation past the budget read all its frames again from its file at every look (each scroll step, a sheet closing, the window back after waking) and threw them away, as there was no room to play them: 6 stickers looked at 5 times were 30 whole decodes for nothing, a heap full of garbage ("mostly garbage" eight times after waking on 2026-11-13, when the sync thread ran out of memory). Its room is taken before its frames are read again (`Playing.reserved`), and without room it stays still, read nothing (`OffScreenAnimationUiTest.oneThatCantPlayIsNotReadAgainEachTimeItsLookedAt`, `AnimatedImage.RELOADS`). Take a budget before the work it pays for, never after.
